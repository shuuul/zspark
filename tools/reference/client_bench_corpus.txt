# 014 — Flash Next on EXL3

## Context

Roadmap 6. TensorFold 0.6.5 decodes `turboderp/Qwen3.8-Flash-Next-exl3` (branch `3.05bpw_h5_ng5`: 52 GB of
weights on the GPU, a 32.6 GB n-gram table) on one GPU. Its EXL3 kernels are plain CUDA behind ATen launchers
(`cuda/exl3/decode.cuh`, `linear.cu`, `experts.cu`, `experts_grouped.cuh`, about 1,260 lines), with the format in
`cuda/exl3/format.py` and `docs/recipes/exl3.md`, and the family's loader in `families/qwen4_exp/cuda/exl3.py`,
`exl3_mm.py`, `exl3_pack.py`. Weights are decoded on the fly into `m16n8k16` B fragments; nothing decoded is kept.
No checkpoint is on either Spark yet, and TensorFold has no reference exporter for this path.

What is EXL3 in the pack: attention `q/k/v_proj` with the indexer's projection (stacked) and `o_proj`; DeltaNet
`in_proj_qkv`, `in_proj_z`, `out_proj`; `lm_head`; the MTP head's `fc_embedding`, `fc_hidden` and layer; the 512
routed experts and the shared one as one table, each matrix at its own width. Unquantized and run as fp16: the
hyper-connection mixers, DeltaNet `in_proj_b`/`in_proj_a`, PLE projections. The n-gram table has its own trellis
(a row: one fp16 scale and 160 values), memory-mapped. Norms are stored centred (gamma - 1). The draft head is
requantized from decoded head rows. There is no FP8 on this path.

## Goal and success criteria

- [x] `zspark chat`, `serve`, `generate`, `score` and `compare` take the EXL3 pack as DIR.
- [x] The format reader agrees with TensorFold's numpy reference (`format.py` `unpack`/`dequantize`) on real
      tensors at every width and codebook the pack holds.
- [x] A row's bits depend on no other row; a window gives its serial steps' bits; a drafted reply equals the
      serial one. Prompts run through the decode kernels (TensorFold's prefill GEMM, other bits, is not taken).
- [x] Against TensorFold on the same pack: every exported point within the tolerance `zspark compare` uses, and
      the greedy reply for the two reference prompts.
- [x] Measured and recorded: load time, memory, decode tok/s serial and drafted, a cold prompt, `zspark score`
      on the 5336 tokens beside the NVFP4 checkpoint's 2.1425.

## Scope and non-goals

In scope:

- Format and pack reader; dense and expert kernels with a C ABI; fp16 linears; the n-gram table; centred norms;
  the layout and model wiring; an exporter option for EXL3 references; tests.

Not in scope:

- GLM-5.3-Flash (the next spec of roadmap 6). Two ranks. TensorFold's prefill GEMM and its workspace.

## Decisions

| Date | Decision | Rationale | Affected workstreams |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 2026-10-05 | TensorFold's `__global__` kernels are taken as they are, the launchers rewritten on `zs::View` | They are plain CUDA already; the port's risk is in the bit arithmetic, which should stay TensorFold's | WS-02, WS-03 |
| 2026-10-05 | Prompts use the decode kernels | The rule: a prompt row is the serial step's bits. TensorFold's prompt path differs from its decode path and keeps prompt ends apart for it | WS-04 |
| 2026-10-05 | Developed on spark-a; the pack is downloaded there | spark-b works on spec 015 with the NVFP4 checkpoint it already holds | all |
| 2026-10-05 | The write-back is `bf16(h + branch * inject)`, one rounding, on every checkpoint | TensorFold's exported streams follow it bit for bit and not the two roundings its Triton source reads; NVFP4 and hybrid bits change with it | WS-05 |
| 2026-10-05 | The hybrid's greedy and sampled `primes` bounds go from 32 and 28 to 18 | Row 40 changes its token under noise of 1.5e-4 in 5 seeds of 8; a reply's length of agreement past such a row is not held by the arithmetic | WS-05 |

# 017 — Structured output: grammar-masked sampling and required tool calls

## Context

zspark refuses `response_format`, the `guided_*` fields and `tool_choice: "required"` with a 400
(`src/server/openai.zig`). TensorFold constrains with xgrammar (`engine/grammar.py`: json_object, json_schema,
regex, choice, EBNF): before a window it walks the draft chain through the matcher, cuts the drafts the grammar
rejects and fills a bitmask a kept row; after the forward the disallowed logits are set to -inf and the row is
sampled; the grammar starts after `</think>`. `tool_choice: "required"` is not a grammar there but `CallGate`
(`engine/call_gate.py`): the reply is cut at the first token that is not a call, `<tool_call>` is spliced in and
decoding resumes. xgrammar is C++ under Python; zspark has no Python at runtime.

## Goal and success criteria

- [x] `response_format` `json_object` and `json_schema` (and what specs 016's APIs map onto them) give replies
      that parse and validate, streamed or whole.
- [x] `tool_choice: "required"` and a named function give a tool call.
- [x] With a constraint a drafted reply still equals the serial one, greedy and sampled.
- [x] The cost of a mask a token is measured and recorded.

## Scope and non-goals

In scope:

- The engine, the mask at `draft.Decoder.round` and at the prompt's end, draft pruning, the call gate, the
  request fields, tests.

Not in scope:

- Regex and EBNF from the request unless the chosen engine gives them for free. Constraints in shared rounds
  (spec 018 takes that up).

### 2026-10-05 — WS-01: both engines measured, a recommendation (Claude subagent, branch `spec-017-engine`)

Host only, on dspark's CPU (aarch64, 20 cores); no model was loaded. The decision is the owner's and the
Decisions row stays open: this entry is the comparison and a recommendation.

**Changed.** `src/text/json_schema.zig` (1130 lines and 289 of tests): the Zig matcher's prototype, registered
in `src/main.zig`'s tests and described in `src/text/AGENTS.md`; nothing in the decoder or the server calls it.
The xgrammar build, the C shim and the benchmark stayed in scratch and are not in the repository.

**xgrammar: what it takes.**

- Source: `mlc-ai/xgrammar` at `8262b5c` (main; the version is set by setuptools-scm, TensorFold pins
  `>=0.2.8,<0.3`). Apache-2.0, with a NOTICE ("XGrammar, Copyright (c) 2024 by XGrammar Contributors").
- C++17, 29 translation units, 41,183 lines with its headers. Bundled and header-only: picojson, dlpack (a
  submodule). Optional and off: cpptrace; googletest for its own tests. It needs pthread (a thread pool in the
  compiler) and libstdc++. No Python is needed once `XGRAMMAR_BUILD_PYTHON_BINDINGS` is off; its
  `cmake/config.cmake` sets it on, so a `config.cmake` in the build directory has to say otherwise.
- Build: CMake and g++ 13.3 (`-O3 -flto=auto`), 3.8 s wall and 35 s of CPU, 542 MB peak; `libxgrammar.a` is
  27.3 MB of LTO objects. Without CMake and LTO, as `build.zig` would do it beside nvcc: `g++ -std=c++17 -O3 -c`
  on the 29 files, 9.3 s wall on 20 jobs, 6.2 MB of objects, linked by `zig build-exe` with the system's
  libstdc++; the benchmark so linked gives the same masks and times. On the command line `-lstdc++` is Zig's
  own libc++ and does not link (the objects want libstdc++'s `std::__cxx11`); `libstdc++.so.6` by its path does.
  `zig-out/bin/zspark` already depends on `libstdc++.so.6`.
- Size: the shim with xgrammar as a shared library, 2.54 MB (1.98 MB stripped).
- Surface: there is no C API. The classes are `TokenizerInfo`, `GrammarCompiler`, `CompiledGrammar` and
  `GrammarMatcher`, with a `DLTensor` of int32 words as the mask, and they throw. The shim was 41 lines of
  header and 128 of C++: `zxg_tokenizer_new` (the tokens' bytes, `VocabType::RAW`, the stop ids),
  `zxg_compiler_new`, `zxg_compile_json_schema`, `zxg_compile_ebnf`, `zxg_matcher_new`, `zxg_fill`,
  `zxg_accept`, `zxg_rollback`, `zxg_terminated`, `zxg_reset`, the `_free`s; every exception caught and
  returned as text. Zig 0.17 has no `@cImport`, so the declarations are written by hand.
- It reads what the Zig matcher does not: `$ref`, `anyOf`, `oneOf`, `allOf`, `pattern`, `format`, string and
  number bounds, `prefixItems`, `patternProperties`, `propertyNames`; and regex, EBNF and structural tags, so
  `guided_regex`, `guided_choice` and `guided_grammar` come with it. It also has `TraverseDraftTree` and a
  batch matcher, which TensorFold does not use.

**The Zig matcher: what it covers.** `type` (a name or a list), `properties` with `required` (in the schema's
order, closed), `items`, `enum`, `const`, `minItems`, `maxItems`, `additionalProperties` beside no
`properties`, an object or a value of any shape, nesting, xgrammar's whitespace rule (up to 32). Anything else
is refused at compile with the keyword's name. What covering the rest would cost, estimated, not built:

| Not covered | Cost |
| --- | --- |
| `$ref`, `$defs`, recursion | 60 to 80 lines: nodes are already ids and the frame stack recurses |
| `anyOf`, `oneOf` told apart by the first byte | about 20 lines over the `choice` node that type lists use |
| `anyOf`, `oneOf` in general | 300 to 500 lines: a position becomes a set of stacks, and the cache is redone |
| `pattern`, `format`, `minLength`, `maxLength` | 600 to 1000 lines: a regex to a byte DFA |
| `minimum`, `maximum` on integers | about 150 lines; on numbers, more |
| `additionalProperties` beside `properties`, `patternProperties` | about 100 lines (and the regex for the second) |
| `allOf` | about 100 lines to merge schemas; not general |
| regex, choice and EBNF from the request | the regex engine above for the first two; EBNF is xgrammar rewritten |

Its other limits: `accept` may allocate (frames are only added, about two a byte, and not compacted until the
matcher is reset); the first mask at a new kind of place allocates and walks the vocabulary; grammars that share
a `Vocabulary` must not fill at once.

**Times.** The checkpoint's `tokenizer.json`: 248,077 ids, masks 248,320 bits wide, stops 248046 and 248044.
ReleaseFast for Zig, `-O3` LTO for xgrammar, one thread, each document of each schema fed token by token as
zspark's tokenizer splits it. Once a process: zspark reads `tokenizer.json` in about 130 ms (it does today);
xgrammar's `TokenizerInfo` 51 ms; the Zig `Vocabulary` 41 ms.

| Schema | Steps | xg compile | xg mask mean | xg worst | Zig compile | Zig first pass mean | first worst | Zig warm mean | warm worst | Differing |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| flat: five scalars, four required | 114 | 19 to 33 ms | 13.1 us | 70.7 us | 0.02 ms | 95.5 us | 6494 us | 1.2 us | 7.5 us | 0 |
| nested: objects, arrays of objects, enums | 225 | 5.9 ms | 13.6 us | 67.6 us | 0.03 ms | 22.8 us | 97 us | 1.1 us | 5.2 us | 0 |
| tool: `get_weather` of `reply_cases.json` | 40 | 4.7 ms | 12.1 us | 79.4 us | 0.01 ms | 17.9 us | 127 us | 1.0 us | 3.9 us | 0 |
| typed: every type, an untyped value | 109 | 5.0 ms | 21.9 us | 85.5 us | 0.02 ms | 35.0 us | 452 us | 1.2 us | 6.8 us | 0 |
| object: `{"type": "object"}` (json_object) | 49 | 5.3 ms | 35.0 us | 130.7 us | 0.01 ms | 5.4 us | 119 us | 2.1 us | 4.0 us | 0 |
| bounded: `minItems`, a union, any keys | 62 | 7.0 ms | 15.3 us | 52.8 us | 0.02 ms | 31.3 us | 466 us | 1.0 us | 5.6 us | 0 |
| all | 599 | | 16.8 us | 130.7 us | | 38.0 us | 6494 us | 1.2 us | 7.5 us | 0 |

- "First pass" is a grammar's first documents, when the Zig matcher fills its cache; "warm" is the same
  documents again. The 6.5 ms is the walk of the vocabulary for a string's inside, once a `Vocabulary`, not
  once a schema or a request; after it the worst first visit of a place was 0.47 ms. xgrammar does its like
  work in the compile, which is why its masks have no such step; its first compile carries a one-time cost
  (19 to 33 ms against about 5).
- Memory: xgrammar's `MemorySizeBytes` is 13.9 MB a compiled grammar; the Zig matcher keeps 0 to 138 kB a
  grammar and 299 kB that every grammar shares.
- `accept`: xgrammar 0.6 to 1.1 us mean, 6.6 worst; Zig about 0.1 us, 0.8 worst. Zig with no cache at all
  (every token walked each time): 2.5 to 6 ms a mask, so the cache is the design, not an optimization.
- Either way a mask is far under a decode step. It was not measured inside the decoder (WS-02's criterion).

**Agreement.** Both were driven with the same tokens, and the masks compared whole at every step.

- The documents: 599 steps, every mask the same bit for bit, with xgrammar given the 21 special tokens as
  empty strings (its way to say "special").
- Given the special tokens' text instead, as TensorFold's `TokenizerInfo.from_huggingface` does, xgrammar
  allows 19 of them (`<|im_start|>`, `<|vision_pad|>`, ...) inside a string: on `flat`, 19 steps and 361 bits.
  The Zig matcher never allows a special token. A zspark binding should pass them empty.
- Random walks, a token drawn from the Zig mask each step (300 a schema, to 160 steps): 169,958 steps, 405
  with differing masks, every one right after a byte 0xED. xgrammar takes ED A0..BF (surrogates, encoded);
  the Zig matcher takes well-formed UTF-8 only, on purpose: the reply has to decode. With that one range
  relaxed for the test, 147,590 steps had no difference.
- The policy the masks share (it is xgrammar's): no whitespace around the document, properties in the schema's
  order, no key that is not declared, `-0` not an integer, enum values written compactly.

**Recommendation** (for shuuul to decide). Start WS-02 on the Zig matcher, behind an interface that names no
engine (compile a schema, fill a mask at a position, accept, step back), and keep xgrammar as the measured
fallback rather than link it now.

- For what the spec scopes (json_object, json_schema, tool arguments) the Zig matcher gives xgrammar's masks,
  bit for bit on what was tried, and is the faster once warm; neither is slow enough for speed to decide.
- It adds no dependency: xgrammar is 41 thousand lines of C++ that would be compiled by g++ in `build.zig`,
  bound through a shim that has to catch every exception, with a thread pool and 14 MB a grammar. It builds in
  seconds and is Apache-2.0, so this is a cost, not an obstacle.
- A position in the Zig matcher is an index into frames that never change: a draft tree is walked without
  rollback, and a mask depends on the tokens before it alone, which is the shape the rule and WS-02's "drafted
  equals serial" want. xgrammar gives the same by `rollback`, as TensorFold uses it.
- The price is coverage. Schemas from real clients carry `$ref` and `anyOf` (Pydantic writes both), and the
  matcher refuses them today. `$ref` and the first-byte `anyOf` are cheap and should be the first thing WS-02 or
  WS-03 adds; `pattern` and `format` need a regex engine and are where the Zig path stops being small.
- So: if the owner wants `guided_regex`, `guided_grammar`, `pattern` or the whole of JSON Schema in this spec,
  link xgrammar instead; nothing measured here stands against it, and the shim is 170 lines.

**Not done, not measured.** No model and no GPU: no reply was generated under a mask, and the mask's cost in a
decode round is WS-02's to measure. xgrammar's regex, EBNF and structural-tag paths were compiled into the
library but not run; nor `TraverseDraftTree`. xgrammar was not compared with the Python wheel TensorFold runs
(the same C++, another build). The static build's size inside `zspark` itself was not measured, only the
benchmark's (2.8 MB stripped, with both engines). The cost table above is an estimate. Walks were drawn from
the Zig mask, so a token only xgrammar allows was compared but never followed.

**Evidence.** `zig test src/text/json_schema.zig` (4 tests; with `ZSPARK_FLASH_NEXT` the checkpoint's
vocabulary too: each mask equals the mask made token by token without the cache); `zig build check-specs`.

**Next.** The owner decides the Decisions row. Then WS-02.

### 2026-10-05 — WS-02: masks in the decoder, and the matcher held to an oracle (Claude subagent, branch `spec-017-masks`)

Written by the subagent at the coordinator's instruction. On spark-a; commits a506639, 157b9a4, a4ad46c, 5398154,
1b7068a and this entry's, from main at 4beef04. Not pushed, not merged.

**Changed.**

- `src/text/json_schema.zig`: `$ref` with `$defs`/`definitions` (any pointer into the schema, recursion
  included) and `anyOf`/`oneOf` whose alternatives begin with different bytes; everything else refused by the
  keyword's name. `Engine` implements the interface below. `Validator` is a second reading of a schema for the
  tests. Then the hardening the owner asked for (below).
- `src/text/grammar.zig`: the interface, naming no engine. `Engine.compile(Spec)` gives a `Constraint` with
  `start`, `fill(position, mask)`, `accept(position, token)` (the position after), `ended`. Positions are
  values.
- `src/flash_next/masks.zig`, `draft.zig`, `session.zig`: `Decoder.masks`. `Masks.window` walks the drafts
  through the grammar and cuts the window before the first draft that may not come at its row or that ends the
  reply (TensorFold's `Constraint.window`: a forbidden draft is never run; the drafter is not told). `apply`
  fills row i's mask at the position after the tokens before row i and writes bf16 -inf into the downloaded
  row; `commit` moves to the position after the rows kept. The prompt's last row is row 0 of the first round
  and is masked the same way. With thinking on nothing is masked or cut until the token after `</think>`.
- `src/main.zig`: `zspark chat --schema FILE`, `--json`.
- `tools/reference/grammar_masks.{zig,cc,sh}`, `src/text/testdata/grammar_masks.txt` (155 KB) and
  `grammar_masks_checkpoint.txt` (94 KB).

**Where the mask is applied, and why.** On the host, in the row the sampler is about to read: the sampler is
on the host and downloads one row at a time, so a device mask would add an upload a row and a kernel to the
step's graph for bits the host reads back anyway. Nothing on the device changed, so no graph changed.

**The rule.** A row's mask is a function of the tokens before that row. A serial step is a window of one row
at the same position, so the bits are the same. Tests on the random model (`zig build test
-Dtest-filter=constraint`): `draft.zig` "under a constraint drafted decoding still gives the serial reply,
greedy and sampled, and the reply is the schema's" (several specs and samplings, drafts that are never, always and sometimes wrong, graphed and eager, and
schemas behind a gate),
against `serialConstrained`, a reference that walks the grammar again from its start at every step;
`session.zig` "a session's reply under a constraint is the serial one, the head drafting, and is the schema's
document" (schemas, samplings, draft depths, thinking) and "without a constraint a reply asks the
matcher nothing and is the reply it was" (the matcher's call counter stays 0; tokens, drafts and statistics
equal a session that never saw an engine).

**Decoder mutations, each seen to fail.** The previous row's mask (`apply` reads `rows[row - 1]`): the
`draft.zig` test. No mask in a verify window (only when nothing is drafted): the `draft.zig` and the
`session.zig` tests. The position not advanced after an accepted draft (`commit` from `rows[0]`): the
`draft.zig` test. The thinking gate ignored: both. A forbidden draft not cut: the `session.zig` test.

**The owner's five conditions.**

1. *xgrammar as the oracle, kept as a fixture.* The generator builds against an xgrammar checkout (8262b5c;
   nothing of it is in the repository) and writes xgrammar's mask before every token over 34 schemas: `$ref`
   three ways with recursion, `anyOf`, `oneOf`, a Pydantic model, four tools from the server tests' shapes, a
   strict-mode schema, deep nesting, empty objects and arrays, enums that go on from one another, escapes and
   characters past ASCII in values and names, `const`, numbers (negative, exponent, `-0`, a leading zero
   refused by the mask after `0`), whitespace to the 32-blank limit, five array bounds, maps, typeless schemas.
   Documents are cut three ways (the tokenizer's, the shortest token, a random token) and there are random
   walks. Tiny tokenizer, masks whole: 636 walks, 19726 masks, 19531 xgrammar's bit for bit; in the others
   2154 tokens xgrammar alone takes, each making a surrogate by a scan of the text that does not use the
   machine, and 1619 under the one schema read more narrowly; no token taken that xgrammar refuses. The
   checkpoint's vocabulary, masks as hashes: 302 walks, 5605 masks, 5451 equal, 79 under the narrowed schema,
   75 not held (the text before them ends in an open escape or ED, where a hash cannot say which tokens
   differ). The meant differences, each tested as such: special tokens never allowed (xgrammar handed the
   token's text takes one in a string: one walk each vocabulary); well-formed UTF-8 (ED A0..BF refused); an
   escaped surrogate needs its partner (seven documents xgrammar takes, each refused at the surrogate);
   `minItems`/`maxItems` with neither `type` nor `items` is an array within its bounds where xgrammar takes
   any value. What the comparison found: `"properties": {}` was read as an open object and xgrammar takes
   `{}` alone (fixed to xgrammar's); xgrammar does not read `~1` in a `$ref` pointer (the corpus avoids it; the
   Zig tests keep it); and xgrammar's mask at 8262b5c lacks tokens its own `AcceptToken` takes after an object
   reached through `"$ref": "#"` (`},`, `}]`, `},{` on the checkpoint's vocabulary; not on the same schema
   written without recursion). The generator puts such tokens to the acceptor and writes them into the mask:
   32 in the checkpoint fixture, none in the tiny one. The fixtures are in `src/text/testdata/`, not
   `src/testdata/` (Decisions).
2. *Properties, seeded.* "properties: what the masks take is a valid document, a valid document is taken
   however it is cut, no position is a dead end, and a mask is its prefix's alone": 60 rounds a schema
   on the tiny tokenizer (seed 0x5eed_017), 6 rounds on the checkpoint's. Soundness is judged by
   `Validator`; completeness by documents written from the schema and cut longest, shortest and at random; the
   stop is in the mask exactly when the document is complete; the same prefix reached by another path gives
   the same bits. And "a schema changed at random compiles or is refused...": 4000 changed schemas (each
   outcome asserted more than 400 times), which found that a typeless alternative under `oneOf` let a document pass two

# 020 — The step's and the windows' graphs captured at load

## Context

A drafted reply runs its windows as CUDA graphs, one for each window size, bucket of context and state side
(`forward.Graphs`, `mtp.Graphs`), and each is captured the first time a reply needs it. Spec 014 measured what
that costs on the EXL3 pack (spark-a, `-Doptimize=fast`): a first drafted reply to `sky` runs at 60.5 to 62.8
tok/s and the same reply once its graphs exist at 67.6 to 69.9, against TensorFold's 70.1 to 71.4; a capturing
window costs about 45 ms. Acceptance equals TensorFold's, so the capture is the whole gap. TensorFold captures
its 21 graphs while it loads (its load with them takes 3 to 7 s longer).

## Goal and success criteria

- [x] `chat` and `serve` capture at load the graphs a reply uses, so a first reply captures none: the count of
      graphs captured during a first reply is zero for the reference prompts, shown by a test or a printed count.
- [x] A first drafted reply on the EXL3 pack and on the NVFP4 checkpoint decodes at the speed of a later one,
      within the run-to-run spread; the load's added time and the device memory the graphs hold are recorded.
- [x] No bits change: a reply drafted, serial and eager is the same tokens as before, and every existing test
      passes on both checkpoints.
- [x] Which keys are captured at load is stated and bounded: what is still captured lazily (deep context
      buckets, rare window sizes) and why, with an option to skip the capture for commands that do not decode.

## Scope and non-goals

In scope:

- The capture at load for the step, the verify windows and the draft head, on NVFP4, hybrid and EXL3.
- An option or a rule for commands that should not pay for it (`info`, `compare`, `score`, `--serial --eager`).

Not in scope:

- Choosing the greedy token on the device, and the draft head's 6 ms (spec 014's other candidates).
- Prompt windows' speed on the pack.

Written by the subagent at the coordinator's request. The branch starts at 9174158. spark-a, every GPU command

How. A capture records its launches and runs none, so `prepare` captures a graph without launching it: no
buffer, no state and no cache is written, nothing is reset afterwards, and it may be called at any time. The
one thing it touches on the host is `State.current`, which `compute` reads: it is put on the side being
captured and back. TensorFold's `warm` runs each window before capturing it and resets the state after.

The rule for the keys. For a session whose replies draft up to d a round:

- the step: windows of 1 to d + 1 rows on either DeltaNet state side, 2(d + 1) graphs. d is `--depth` (6) in
  `chat` and `generate`, and `ReplyOptions.depth` (15) in `serve`, which is what a request's reply runs with;
- the head, 48 whatever d is, because a prompt's last window is as wide as the buffers score (16 rows): 1 to
  16 rows of the main model's streams absorbed, the last scored or not (a round's kept rows, a prompt's last
  windows, a reply's end), 32 graphs; and a chained draft's one row read from each of the 16 rows of the head's
  own streams, 16 graphs (the key holds the address the streams are read from);
- over the first bucket of context only (`session.prepared_buckets` = 1): sequences that end within 8192
  tokens, or the context where it is smaller (`chat`'s 4096).

So 62 graphs in `chat` and `generate` (14 and 48; TensorFold's 21 are 14 and 7, its head key being the rows
alone) and 80 in `serve` (32 and 48). Left to the window that first needs one: the buckets past 8192 (16384,
32768, ... up to `--context`: as many graphs again at most for each, entered once a doubling, so six buckets
at 262,144 tokens; captured at load they would be 0.15 to 0.25 s and 130 to 180 MiB each, for contexts most
sessions never reach); a prompt's windows, which run eagerly as before; and a reply asked for more drafts than
the session prepared (`Session.reply` with a deeper `depth`). Who does not pay: a session without a head
(`--serial`, `--depth 0`) prepares the one-row step's two graphs; `--eager` none; `--lazy-graphs` (`generate
... lazy`) none, each graph captured by its first window as before; `info`, `compare`, `score`, `trace`,
serial `generate` and the tests' `compare.Bench` make no session and are unchanged.

**Finding B: `serve`'s 15 is a struct field's default, not a decision; one default of 6 for both is what the
numbers support.**

Where the two numbers come from. `chat --depth` (default `draft_depth` 6) arrived with the MTP head in b0d568a
(spec 005, which names TensorFold's `DEPTH` 6 and `CONFIDENCE` 0.7 as the rule being ported). 2dea901 (spec

`Chat.reply`, the server's backend, passes none, so a request drafts to 15. No spec of 005 to 008 records a
choice of 15 for `serve`, no commit message mentions it, and `README.md` says "up to six". TensorFold serves
`qwen4_exp` with `DEPTH = 6` (`families/qwen4_exp/cuda/__init__.py`; `MAX_DEPTH = 15` is the window's limit in
`engine.py`), changed only by `--mtp-drafts N`; it does not adapt it: `mtp_decode` asks for `min(depth, tokens
left)` each round and a chain stops before a draft under 0.7, as zspark's does.
